The 40Hz GENUS Protocol (MIT Picower Institute)

Researchers at MIT's Picower Institute for Learning and Memory, led by Li-Huei Tsai, discovered that sensory stimulation at exactly 40Hz — the brain's gamma frequency — can drive neural oscillations, reduce amyloid and tau proteins associated with Alzheimer's disease, and improve cognitive function.

GENUS (Gamma Entrainment Using Sensory Stimuli) uses synchronized light flickering and auditory tones at 40Hz. Phase II clinical trials showed significant slowing of brain atrophy and improved functional connectivity in participants with mild Alzheimer's dementia after daily 1-hour sessions.

Audio Modalities Explained

Monaural beats: A single 40Hz amplitude-modulated tone delivered to both ears — simpler, no headphones required. The MIT GENUS protocol uses a 40Hz click train (pure auditory pulse), but amplitude-modulated tones are an accessible analog.

Binaural beats: Two slightly different tones (e.g., 440Hz left, 480Hz right) produce a 40Hz perceived beat inside the brain. Requires stereo headphones. Carrier frequency of 340-400Hz has shown strongest gamma entrainment in parametric studies.

Isochronic tones: Rapid on/off pulses at exactly 40Hz. Can be used with or without headphones. Some studies show stronger cortical entrainment than binaural beats.

Recommended Practice

MIT clinical studies used 1 hour daily of synchronized audiovisual stimulation. Shorter sessions (20–30 min) may provide partial benefit. Consistent daily use over weeks is associated with measurable changes in gamma oscillation power and coherence.

→ Sources: MIT Picower Institute (2019–2025), Iaccarino et al. Nature 2016, PMC9714926 Phase II Clinical Trial
